# MRD Outline

Source: Kellogg PM101 "MRD Deliverable Outline" template. Use this before building anything real — it's about whether the problem and market are worth solving, not about the solution's design.

## Sections

1. **Vision** — 1–2 sentences: target customer, their unmet need, your proposed solution. Use Geoffrey Moore's positioning statement (*Crossing the Chasm*): "For [target customer segment] who must [problem to be solved], our product is a new [category] that provides [solution]. Unlike [current solutions], we offer [key differentiator]."
2. **Motivation** — why this, why us.
3. **Personas** — primary and secondary. How do early adopters differ from mainstream users?
4. **Unmet Needs** — for each persona, the hypothesis format: *"I believe [customer segment] experiences [problem] when doing [task]"* or *"I believe [customer segment] experiences [problem] because of [constraint]."* Include how big the pain actually is, and what evidence (research design, sample size, results) backs the hypothesis.
5. **Existing Solutions** — what customers rely on today, and hypotheses on where those fall short. A competitor feature matrix if relevant.
6. **Differentiation** — why we're best equipped to pursue this specific opportunity.
7. **Why Now?** — what's changed, internally or externally, that makes this available now.
8. **Use Scenarios** — step-by-step narrative of a persona experiencing an ideal solution. No UI details, no wireframes, no specific technology — just "is this feasible with existing technology" (no anti-gravity ray guns). Vary by persona if needed.
9. **Market Size** — is the total addressable market (TAM) big enough to justify building this? TAM = size of the whole market for the need, *not* projected revenue for your product. Don't overdo precision here — new markets have blurry boundaries; the point is "big enough," not exact.
10. **Adoption Barriers** — are priorities aligned across everyone in the purchase decision? What switching costs do users face leaving their current solution? How much does vendor reputation matter to early adopters?
11. **Risks / Key Dependencies** — privacy, legal/patent, piracy, dependencies on internal systems, dependencies on third parties. Table format: risk, description, mitigant.
12. **Strategic Considerations** — for products built inside an existing org: does this fit the org's strategy and complement existing products? (Skip for independent/solo projects.)
13. **Team Members** — who's working on this and their role. For solo builds, this is just you (and me, as the build partner).
14. **Go/No-Go Recommendation** — the actual call, made explicitly, not left implicit.
15. **Customer Research Data Appendices** — raw research material: interview guides, notes, survey questions/results, and how the sample was selected.

## Right-sizing for smaller projects

Full MRD is for something with real stakes — a business idea, a thing meant to acquire real users or money. For a toy project or a "labs" entry, trim to just: **Vision, Unmet Needs, Existing Solutions, Use Scenarios, Go/No-Go** — skip market sizing, adoption barriers, and the research appendix unless the project has grown into something that actually warrants them.
